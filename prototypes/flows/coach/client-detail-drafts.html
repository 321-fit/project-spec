Coach · draft A

A · Focus card

Bet: the coach opened this for one thing, and it is almost always the next session or the money. Everything else is reference and can afford a tap.

The live screen stacks 12 blocks of equal weight, three of them horizontal carousels. Nothing is foregrounded, so it reads as a table rotated on its side. This draft takes the opposite position: one hero, one line of live state, five flat rows.

  • The hero is the next session, at the size that says the screen was opened for it. Two actions on it, not five.
  • The live row is the whole idea: anything with a state (money owed, reviews due, a pack running out, group membership) is a chip on one scrollable line. Anything without a state is a record, and records go in the list below.
  • All three carousels are gone. A carousel says "homogeneous data, keep swiping" — the opposite of "here is what matters".
  • Rows carry their own count (3 upcoming, €70 due, 1 of 3, 47), so the list is scannable without opening anything.
What it costs: a coach who wants to compare next week's three sessions has to open a screen to do it. If that turns out to be a frequent job, A is wrong.

Liveliness here is restrained on purpose: staggered entry and a press spring. The energy is in the hero's colour, not in motion.

Coach · draft B

B · Timeline

Bet: a coaching relationship is a story, and people read stories as a feed. Categories are the app's problem, not the coach's.

Money, training and self-paced stop being three sections and become three kinds of event on one spine. That is what actually kills the carousels — not restyling them, but removing the reason they existed.

  • Chronology instead of categories. "Bought a pack", "sent a clip", "no-show" and "next session" sit together because that is how they happened.
  • Colour on the spine dot carries the kind: blue upcoming, amber money, teal good, red trouble. No section headers needed.
  • Actions live on the event — Review on the clip, Mark paid on the no-show. The live screen makes you find the right section first.
  • Liveliness is free here. A feed has a natural loading rhythm; the stagger reads as the story assembling rather than as decoration.
What it costs: "how much do they owe in total" is no longer a place, it is a pill you have to trust. A feed is bad at aggregates — that is why the three pills sit pinned at the top, and if they turn out to carry too much, B is wrong.
Coach · draft C

C · Segments

Bet: the mess comes from three unrelated domains sharing one scroll. Give each a pane and the screen stops competing with itself.
  • The hero is the emotional one — ring that draws on entry, counters that run up, a soft brand glow. This is the draft that answers "not enough life" most directly.
  • Training / Money / About — three panes, and the split is real: those are three different jobs on three different days.
  • Every pane is a flat list of equal cards, which is fine inside a domain and was the problem only across domains.
What it costs: two thirds of the screen is behind a tab, and tabs hide badly — a coach with €70 outstanding sees nothing about it while on Training. The obvious patch (a badge on the tab) starts rebuilding the noise we removed.

Also worth noticing: the stats hero is charming but it is vanity — 47 sessions and 8 months do not change what the coach does next. A and B spend that space on the next session instead. Whether that trade is right is the actual question this draft asks.

Coach · draft D

D · Accent budget

Bet: the screen does not feel dirty because it holds too much. It feels dirty because one colour is doing five jobs, so nothing on the screen is primary.

Counted across flows/: brand-primary 244×, teal-500 457×, brand-gradient 129×. On coach/dashboard.html alone the same brand paints a link, a metric value, an icon plate, a chip and the CTA — five meanings, one colour. Put a brand-filled card and a brand-filled button on one screen and they physically compete: the eye cannot pick a winner.

This draft is A with one rule, nothing else changed:

  • One brand-filled surface per screen, and it is the primary action. The hero loses its gradient; the footer CTA keeps it.
  • Emphasis moves to type and a 3px rule. "Tomorrow · 10:00" is still the loudest thing on the screen — by size and weight, not by fill.
  • State keeps its own language: red owes, amber needs review, teal is fine. Those are semantic, not brand — and they stay readable now that they are not shouting over a gradient.
  • The avatar stops being brand-coloured. A person is not an accent.
What it costs: the screen is calmer and, on first glance, flatter — the hero no longer announces itself with colour, so it has to earn attention with hierarchy. If the next session stops being found instantly, the rule is too strict and the hero gets a tinted (not filled) surface back.

If this direction wins it becomes a token contract, not a habit: accent = action, semantic = state, everything else neutral.

Coach · draft E

E · Media grammar

Bet: the tabular feel is not a layout problem. Every object wears the same 40px icon plate, so every row looks like every other row — a picture is what makes a row recognisable before it is read.

Across 830 screens in the prototypes there are 18 <img> tags, in 3 files. fit-ui.css has .fit-avatar and no media primitive at all — no cover, no tile, no scrim. The icon was never chosen as a style; it is the only thing that exists.

The rule this draft proposes, and the reason it needs no fallback machinery:

  • A person → avatar. Circle, initials fallback, unchanged. Faces are user content: unpredictable crop, quality and moderation. Anna stays a 44px circle.
  • A thing → a picture. Sessions, locations, groups — content we control. The sport taxonomy is closed (33 sports / 8 sections), so a curated library keys off the slug and can never miss; a location is a static map render from its coordinates; a group is a collage.
  • A concept → keeps its plate. Money, notes, history, settings, sync. There is no honest picture of "€70 due", and inventing one is decoration.

Because the imagery is ours and deterministic, the only fallback the system needs is the one it already has — initials on a tint, for people.

What it costs: a cover is ~170px of screen for one object, so fewer rows are visible at once. And a picture that is not about this client (a stock tennis court, not her court) can read as filler — the library has to be good, or the plate was more honest.

The four images here are generated stand-ins for layout testing: they prove the grammar (scrim, aspect, text-over-image, tile in place of plate), not the photography.

Coach · draft F · recommended

F · Both, in light

Bet: the two fixes are one fix. Once a card gets its contrast from material, the brand has nothing left to do there — and the accent contract stops being a restriction and becomes what is left over.

Same screen as E, wearing D's rule, opened in light on purpose: light is where the mud was reported, and where a scrim, a white surface and a brand fill are hardest to keep apart. Toggle Dark in the sidebar — it has to survive both.

  • Exactly one brand fill: Book a session. The cover carries the hero with an image and a scrim instead.
  • Three visual jobs, three grammars: image = a thing, plate = a concept, circle = a person. A row's kind is now legible before its label.
  • Rhythm, not just pictures: one large object then a list. Six identical rows is what reads as a table — varying the size breaks it as much as the imagery does.
What it costs: two new primitives to build and keep (fit-media--cover, fit-media--tile, plus scrim and aspect tokens, and their native twins) and an asset library that someone has to curate and host. That is the real bill for this direction — not the CSS.

If F is the direction, the next step is not more drafts: it is lab/components.html gaining the two media specimens and the accent contract written into theme-contrast.md, so the rebuild has something to follow.

Coach · draft G

G · Floating panels

Bet (Revolut): colour belongs in the air, not on the controls — and a list stops looking like a table the moment you delete every line in it.

Three borrowings, each answering something we complained about:

  • The brand became a wash. A radial tint sits behind the top third and fades to nothing. The screen reads as branded with zero saturated rectangles — which is the cleanest possible answer to "we overuse the main colour".
  • Actions became circles. Note what this is: .fit-action-circles / FitActionCircle, already in the design system, currently used only for accept/decline. Revolut's row is the same component doing a bigger job — nothing new to build.
  • No hairlines at all. Rows inside a panel are held apart by 12px of air and a shared left rhythm. This is most of what reads as "floatiness"; the rest is 22px radius and one surface step.
  • The header emptied out. Back and two circular utilities, nothing else — identity moved into the content where it can be centred and given room.
What it costs: without rules, rows need height and disciplined alignment or they turn to mush — Revolut can afford it because their screens are sparse. Ours are not: a client with three packs, two groups and a debt will push those panels long. And there is no CTA at all here: booking is a circle, which is quieter than a footer button. If booking is the job, that is a real loss.
Coach · draft H

H · Big title + tiles

Bet (Uber): the header is overloaded because it is trying to be identity, state and next action at once. Let the name be the header, at title size, and give every action its own tile.
  • The name is the screen title, two lines, 32px, avatar to the right — exactly Uber's account screen. Nothing else competes up there.
  • A 2×2 grid of destinations instead of five identical rows. Peers of equal weight sit side by side; each carries its own state line ("€70 owed", "2 unread") and a dot when it needs attention.
  • The action rides the row — Mark paid, Review, Reschedule as outline pills, Uber's "Rebook". A chevron says "go find it"; a pill says "do it here".
  • Section headers are tappable ("Recent ›"), so a section can show three items instead of everything — that is how these apps stay short.
What it costs: a 2×2 grid is a fixed budget — four destinations, no more, so a fifth one has to displace something or start a second grid that undoes the effect. And large-title screens set an expectation the rest of the app has to keep: one screen with a 32px name among twenty with 17px headers looks like a different app, not a better one.

Both G and H drop the footer CTA on purpose. Worth watching whether the screen still answers "book a session" fast enough — if not, the answer is probably H's tile grid plus a footer, not a return to the old header.

Coach · draft I · G reworked

I · Floating, filled

Bet: G's language is right, but it was proven on an empty screen. This is the same language on a client who actually has a life — a debt, a pack, three things this week, two groups, a clip to review.

What changed from G, point by point:

  • The wash starts above the status bar. The black band under the phone chrome was a leak, not a design — the gradient now owns the whole top of the device and the header floats in it.
  • The header is chrome only — back, message, ⋯. No title, no state, no session.
  • Identity got the room it earns: 84px avatar, the name at 28px. This is a screen about a person; the person should be the largest thing on it.
  • The next session left the header and became the first section under the circles, with its own actions. And when there is nothing booked, the same slot carries the CTA ("no next session" in the sidebar toggles it) — so the primary action never disappears, it just changes shape. That is also the only moment a brand-filled button exists on this screen.
  • Sections came back from D — a title outside the panel, a chevron into the full list, and a count on the right where a count means something ("€70 owed", "3 sessions").
  • One section is a tile grid, not a list (Money as two tiles, More as four). Same data, different grammar — and it breaks the single long column that made every draft feel like a table.
What it costs: the screen is now long — five sections deep, which is the honest length for this much state, but it means the circles scroll away and the coach loses the actions after one flick. Worth testing a sticky circle row, or accepting that "Book" also lives in the Next-session slot. And the tile grid caps at four: a fifth thing under More either replaces one or reopens the list.

Density on purpose: the earlier drafts looked calm because Anna had two rows. This is the version to judge — a language that only holds at bare minimum will break the first time a real client opens.
