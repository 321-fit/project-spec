1 · Event drawer

A personal session with a second person is a kind, not a status. The six statuses are untouched; what changes is how many seats the event has.

The drawer gains one block: Participants, with the seat count and + Add participant. It is the same roster component the group drawer uses (.fit-participant) — and it renders at one seat too. A one-row list is a normal list; two layouts for the same question ("who is coming?") is a tax on the coach.

  • The title stays personal — "Tennis · 60 min", never "Group session". Kind changes the block, not the name.
  • Cap is 3. Beyond that it is honestly a group session, with a minimum, a join link and a chat.
  • On the calendar the tile stays teal (personal). A second person shows as a stacked-avatar pair — the two-colour type language does not get a third colour.

Spec: specs/semi-private.md §6.

2 · Participants & price

This is the existing instance editor (calendar.html#s-event-edit), not a new screen. Its single Price field grows into seats; Location, Duration and Note are untouched. Both the drawer’s + Add participant and Edit details land here.

  • Anchor is the original athlete. The event cannot lose them — removing the anchor is cancelling the session, not editing it.
  • Each seat shows its own price and its own state (re-confirms / invited), because money here is per person.
  • The outline note states the rule in the coach’s words: adding a person or raising a price asks the athlete again; lowering a price alone asks nothing — there is nothing to refuse about a discount.
Open: naming. "Add participant" describes it exactly; "Extend session" also covers a price bump with no second person. The screen currently says the first.
Coach

3 · Who to add

The invite picker from invite.html#s-invite-select, reused — own clients and CRM contacts, search, already-on-this-session rows disabled.

One row is deliberately missing: invite by link. A group session is something people can join; a personal session with a second seat is something the coach fills. No link, no self-join, no request-to-join.

A CRM contact can take a seat, but has no app account to confirm with — the coach confirms it on their behalf (the proxy-accept that already exists for cash invites), and the row says so before it is picked.

Coach

4 · Seat editor

Everything that belongs to one person: their price, their payment method, and what extra they asked for. The seat table already stores payment per participant, so this is not new plumbing — it is that plumbing, exposed.

  • An extra is its own line (label + amount), never an overwrite of the price. Overwrite it and neither the receipt nor Earnings can explain why a €50 session cost €65.
  • The total is stated — "Piotr pays €65" — because two numbers on a screen invite a wrong assumption about which one is charged.
  • A pack credit cannot pay an extra. The credit was sold for a session; the €15 needs a second method, and the review drawer blocks the save until it has one.
Open: free-text extras (fast, but Earnings fills with near-duplicate labels) or a catalogue the coach maintains in settings (tidy, but a new entity and a new screen). Drawn as free text.
Coach

5 · Before saving

Same grammar as the existing conflict drawer (calendar.html#cal-overlap-sheet): a severity badge, the list, one line of consequence, and actions named after what they do.

  • Home visit is the one that matters most. If the session is at Anna’s address, adding Piotr discloses where she lives. That is her privacy, not a detail — so it is a blocking item with its own checkbox, not a line of grey text.
  • A busy invitee does not block. Piotr decides for himself; the coach only needs to know before sending.
  • Money warnings that do block: a pack credit facing an extra, and a card hold that no longer covers the total.
  • The footer states the mechanism once: two people, two deadlines, one held slot.
Athlete

6 · Re-confirmation

Decline is not a cancellation. It reverts the session to the terms she agreed to — tomorrow 10:00, alone, €50. She did not do anything wrong; the coach proposed a change and she said no.

Shown in light, the athlete default. Three things carry the screen:

  • was → now, for both composition and price. A re-confirmation that does not say what changed is just a button.
  • Who she is training with — name and photo, decided deliberately: they will stand on the same court.
  • Only her own money. Piotr’s row has no price. The coach may have given one of them a discount, and that is not the other’s business.

The secondary button is worded as the outcome (Keep it as it was), not as a refusal (Decline) — it is the more accurate of the two, and it removes the fear that saying no loses the booking.

She never sees the term "semi-private". For her this is "Tennis, with Piotr".
