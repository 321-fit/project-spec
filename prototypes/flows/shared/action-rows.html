Action rows — the inventory

Built by scanning every prototype file for elements that directly contain two or more <button>: 446 rows. Grouped by the question the row answers, not by how it looks, they fall into six families — and the grouping is the finding: the largest family by far is "are you sure?", ~40 rows, and none of it should change. A styling sweep that did not make that distinction would have turned destructive confirmations into glyphs.

The rule this produced: peer actions on an object want circles; a question with an escape wants words. Circles start earning their place at three actions — at two, a wide pair still reads as a fork and has room for the labels.

Nothing here is decided. Walk the six screens and mark what changes; the two amber notes are the ones where I need a product answer rather than a styling one.

Shared

1 · Decide on an incoming thing

Nine places, all the same shape: a request arrived, it wants an answer, and the card ends in red + gradient. Coach dashboard requests, athlete inbox "To reply", both calendars' event sheets, self-paced inbox, the voice-assistant cards.

Open question (amber): two actions is exactly where wide buttons still work. These become circles only if they gain a third — Reschedule, or Ask AI from the assistant proposal. That is a product decision about what the user may do with a request, not a styling one.

Shared

2 · Actions on an object

The case the assistant proposal already answered, and the one that generalises: not a question but a menu of what you may do to the thing in front of you. Three to five peers, each with a real icon, none of them "the escape".

Colour is rationed and that is the transferable part: exactly one gradient (the expected answer, under the thumb), exactly one red (destructive, farthest from it), the assistant outlined only. A third fill turns four actions into four warnings.

Where this shape belongs beyond the event drawer: the group-event drawer, the client card sheet, and the self-paced review sheet — all currently stacks of full-width buttons.

Shared

3 · Outcome pairs

My read: leave them. The two options are not peers — one is the expected outcome, the other the exception — and the labels carry meaning no icon does. "Missed" as a circle is a guess. Listed because they look like family 1 and would be swept up by a careless pass.

The exception: if the review queue ever grows a third outcome (late, partial), it stops being a pair and becomes family 2.

Shared

4 · Are you sure? — not circles

~40 rows: every Disconnect/Cancel, every Cancel/Save editor sheet, every destructive confirmation. One question, one answer, one escape.

Why not: the words are the meaning. "Disconnect" has no icon, and a destructive confirmation is the last place to make someone decode a glyph. Cancel/Save is a commit and a way out, not two peers.

Shared

5 · Pick one of many — not circles

Action sheets: unrelated choices, each needing a sentence. "Confirm for athlete" is not a glyph. A list is already the right shape — it scrolls, it takes subtitles, and it does not run out of horizontal room at five items, which a circle row does.

Shared

6 · One action alone — not circles

A circle needs peers; alone it is decoration.

Open question (amber): the athlete's Awaiting state is the one place where the user can do nothing but wait and the only button is destructive ("Cancel request"). Giving it Message coach and Ask AI would make it three peers — family 2 — and would turn a dead end into a screen with something to do. Product question.
